Dental Lead Response Guide

Missed call text back
for dental offices

What an automatic text after a missed call does, when to send it, and 8 short templates you can copy, plus the mistakes that make it feel like spam.

By Usman Ali, founder of CRM Flow Lab · Published November 11, 2026

Quick answer: Missed call text back is an automatic text that goes out within seconds of a call your front desk could not take. It says you saw the call, asks one simple question and links to online booking. It works because a patient who just called is still ready to book. We build it as part of instant lead response.

A dental front desk can’t answer every call. Someone is checking a patient in, someone is on the other line, and after 5 p.m. nobody is there at all. Some of those callers don’t leave a voicemail. They hang up and may call the next practice on the list.

A text back fixes the gap without adding staff. Below is how it works, what to put in the message, and eight templates we use as starting points. Replace the placeholders in curly brackets and keep the wording close to how your team actually talks.

How does missed call text back work?

The practice phone number is connected to a CRM. When a call isn’t answered, the CRM creates or updates the contact and sends a text from the same number. From the patient’s side, it looks like the office replied a few seconds after they hung up.

  1. A call is missed during a busy period, at lunch or after hours.
  2. A text goes out within seconds, from the practice number.
  3. The patient replies or books through the link in the text.
  4. The front desk sees the thread in one inbox, with the contact already created, and picks up anything that needs a person.

When should a dental office text back after a missed call?

Speed matters more than polish. A patient with a toothache or a cracked filling wants an answer now. Send the text straight away rather than batching missed calls for someone to deal with later.

Use different wording by situation, because a call at 2 p.m. and a call at 9 p.m. are not the same:

  • Open hours: a short “sorry we missed you, how can we help?” text with a booking link.
  • After hours: say when the office reopens and offer online booking in the meantime.
  • Pain or urgent calls: point to your emergency instructions, and flag the thread so a person replies first thing.
  • Repeat callers: if the same number calls twice, notify the front desk instead of sending a second text.

Missed call text back templates

1. During office hours

Hi, this is {practice}. Sorry we missed your call. How can we help? You can book online here: {booking_link}, or reply and we’ll get back to you shortly.

2. After hours

Thanks for calling {practice}. We’re closed right now and reopen {open_time}. Want us to call you back? Reply with a good time, or book here: {booking_link}

3. Lunch or busy period

Hi, {practice} here. We’re with patients and couldn’t pick up. What can we help with? Reply here and a team member will answer as soon as they’re free.

4. New patient caller

Thanks for calling {practice}! Are you looking to book a first visit? You can pick a time here: {booking_link}. New patients are welcome.

5. Possible emergency

Hi, this is {practice}. If you have severe pain, swelling or bleeding, reply URGENT or call {emergency_line}. For anything else, book here: {booking_link}

6. Existing patient

Hi {first_name}, we missed your call at {practice}. Is it about an appointment? Reply C to confirm, R to reschedule, or tell us what you need.

7. Follow-up if there’s no reply

Hi, {practice} again. We didn’t want your call to go unanswered. Still need help? Book here: {booking_link} or reply and we’ll call you.

8. Voicemail left

Thanks for your message, {first_name}. We got your voicemail at {practice} and someone will call you back by {callback_time}. Want to skip the wait? {booking_link}

Why does a fast reply matter for a dental practice?

A patient who is shopping around may call more than one office. A caller who reaches voicemail has not decided anything yet, so the practice that responds in seconds has a head start. We don’t quote a conversion figure here, because it depends on your call volume and how many calls go unanswered. The easiest way to see your own number is to count missed calls for one week, then check how many of those callers ever booked.

It also changes how the front desk works. Instead of working through a voicemail list at 8 a.m. and playing phone tag, staff open an inbox where the patient has already answered the first question.

What makes a missed call text feel like spam?

Most problems come from over-sending or being vague. Watch for these:

  • Sending more than one text. One text, plus at most one follow-up. Then stop and let the front desk call.
  • Asking several questions. One question and one link is enough.
  • Mentioning treatment. Don’t reference procedures or conditions in an automated text.
  • Texting patients who already called back. Cancel the sequence the moment the call is returned or the patient replies.
  • Ignoring opt-outs. Honor STOP right away, and run US business texting on a registered number.
