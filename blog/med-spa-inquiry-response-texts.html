Med Spa Lead Response Guide

Med spa inquiry response
6 texts to send in 60 seconds

Why the first reply matters, six texts you can adapt, a short follow-up sequence, and how to automate it day and night.

By Usman Ali, founder of CRM Flow Lab · Published November 11, 2026

Quick answer: Reply to every new med spa inquiry by text within a minute, day or night. Greet the client by first name, answer the one thing they asked about and include a booking link so they can pick a consult time. An automation sends that first reply for you, then follows up gently and hands over to a person as soon as the client writes back.

A Botox or filler inquiry often arrives in the evening, after the front desk has gone home. If nobody answers until the next morning, the client may already have booked somewhere else. We don’t quote conversion figures here, because results depend on your spa, your treatments and your market. The principle is simple: the first spa to answer usually has the advantage.

Why does the first reply matter so much?

  • Interest is highest at the moment of inquiry. The client is on their phone, comparing spas right now.
  • Evenings and weekends are when many inquiries arrive. A team that works office hours will miss them.
  • A fast reply shows what the experience will be like. It tells the client you are organized and attentive.

What inquiry response texts can you copy?

Replace the bracketed parts with your own details. Keep treatment and health details out of the text itself.

1. Instant first reply.
“Hi [First name], thanks for contacting [Spa name]! We’d love to help. You can see our available consult times and book here: [Booking link]”

2. After-hours reply.
“Hi [First name], thanks for reaching out to [Spa name]. Our team is away right now, but you can book a consult any time here: [Booking link]. We’ll be in touch when we reopen.”

3. Reply to an Instagram or Facebook message.
“Hi [First name], thanks for your message! The easiest way to get started is a consult. Pick a time that suits you: [Booking link]”

4. Nudge if there is no reply.
“Hi [First name], just checking in. Would you like help choosing a consult time? Here is the link again: [Booking link]”

5. Final gentle follow-up.
“Hi [First name], we don’t want to bother you, so this is our last note. If you’d like to book later, our link is always here: [Booking link]”

6. Booking confirmation with deposit.
“You’re booked, [First name]! Your consult is on [Day, time]. Your deposit has been received. Reply here if you need to reschedule.”

What should the follow-up sequence look like?

  • Minute one: the instant reply with the booking link.
  • A later nudge: one friendly reminder if the client has not booked or replied.
  • One last note: then stop. Do not keep messaging someone who is not responding.
  • Stop rules: end the sequence the moment the client books, replies or opts out.

What should you check before automating replies?

  • Consent. Make sure inquiry forms make clear that the client may receive texts, and honor opt-outs straight away.
  • Privacy. Do not mention treatments or health details in the text. Check your obligations with your own advisor.
  • A human handoff. When a client replies with a question, a person should answer.
  • One clear action. Each text should ask for one thing: usually, booking a consult.

How do you automate inquiry response?

Manual replies depend on someone seeing the message in time. With a CRM the process runs on its own:

  • Capture inquiries from your website, forms, texts, Instagram and Facebook in one inbox.
  • Reply automatically by text in under 60 seconds, including after hours.
  • Share a booking page with real-time availability and a deposit at booking.
  • Follow up with a short sequence that stops when the client books or replies.
  • Alert your team when a client writes back so a person can step in.
