Dental Reminders Guide

Dental appointment reminder
text templates

14 short, ready-to-send texts for confirmations, 48-hour and same-day reminders, new patients and missed appointments, plus when to send each one and how to keep them private.

By Usman Ali, founder of CRM Flow Lab · Published November 6, 2026

Quick answer: A good dental appointment reminder text names the practice, the day and time, and gives one clear action: reply C to confirm or tap a link to reschedule. Send a confirmation when the appointment is booked, a reminder 48 hours before, and a short one on the morning of the visit. Keep clinical details out of the text and include an opt-out.

Most missed dental appointments aren’t patients deciding not to come. They forgot, the day got away from them, or rescheduling meant calling during work hours. A reminder that arrives at the right time, and makes confirming or moving the appointment take a few seconds, fixes most of that.

Below are the templates we use as starting points when we set up reminder sequences for dental clinics. Replace the placeholders in curly brackets, and keep the wording close to how your front desk actually talks.

When should a dental practice send appointment reminders?

One reminder the day before is easy to miss and leaves no time to refill the chair. A short sequence works better:

  • At booking: a confirmation with the date, time and address.
  • 48 hours before: the reminder that matters most, with confirm and reschedule options. If the patient can’t make it, there’s still time to offer the slot to someone else.
  • Morning of: a one-line “see you at 2:30” text.
  • Long-lead bookings: for hygiene visits booked six months ahead, add a reminder about a week out, because patients forget appointments made that far in advance.

Booking confirmation texts

1. The standard confirmation

Hi {first_name}, you’re booked at {practice} on {date} at {time}. Address: {address}. Need to change it? {reschedule_link}

2. The six-month hygiene booking

Hi {first_name}, your next cleaning at {practice} is booked for {date} at {time}. We’ll send a reminder closer to the day. Reply STOP to opt out.

3. The new patient welcome

Welcome to {practice}, {first_name}! Your first visit is {date} at {time}. Please fill in your forms before you arrive: {forms_link}. Parking: {parking_note}

48-hour reminder texts

This is the reminder that saves the appointment. Always give the patient an easy way to say yes and an easy way to move it.

4. Reply-to-confirm

Hi {first_name}, reminder from {practice}: you’re booked for {day} at {time}. Reply C to confirm or R to reschedule.

5. Link-based confirm

Hi {first_name}, see you {day} at {time} at {practice}. Confirm here: {confirm_link}. Can’t make it? Pick a new time: {reschedule_link}

6. Family booking (several patients in one visit)

Hi {first_name}, reminder: {practice} has {count} appointments booked for your family on {day} starting at {time}. Reply C to confirm or call {phone} to change.

7. Unconfirmed follow-up

Hi {first_name}, we haven’t heard back about your visit on {day} at {time}. Are you still able to come? Reply C to confirm or R and we’ll find another time.

Same-day reminder texts

8. Morning-of

Good morning {first_name}! See you today at {time} at {practice}. Running late? Just reply here.

9. Arrival instructions

Hi {first_name}, your appointment is at {time} today. Please arrive 10 minutes early and bring your insurance card if anything has changed. – {practice}

Rescheduling and missed appointment texts

10. After a reschedule request

No problem, {first_name}. Here are the next open times at {practice}: {booking_link}. Or reply with a day that suits you and we’ll sort it.

11. Same-day missed appointment

Hi {first_name}, we missed you at {practice} today. Hope everything’s okay. Want to pick a new time? {booking_link}

12. Second follow-up (3–5 days later)

Hi {first_name}, just checking in from {practice}. Your visit still needs rebooking. It takes about 30 seconds here: {booking_link}

Short-notice opening texts

When someone cancels inside 48 hours, a quick text to patients who asked for an earlier slot can fill the chair.

13. Waitlist offer

Hi {first_name}, a {time} slot just opened at {practice} on {day}. Want it? First to book gets it: {booking_link}

14. Earlier-appointment offer

Hi {first_name}, you asked about an earlier visit. We have {day} at {time} available. Reply YES to take it and we’ll move your booking.

How do you keep reminder texts private?

Appointment reminders are routine, but they still go to a phone other people may see. A few rules keep them safe:

  • Say when, not why. Name the practice, day and time. Leave out procedures, diagnoses and treatment details.
  • Get consent first. Collect opt-in on your forms and booking pages, and honor STOP replies straight away.
  • Register your number. In the US, business texting should run on an A2P 10DLC registered number.
  • Respect patient requests. If a patient asks to be contacted a different way, note it and follow it.

The US Department of Health and Human Services says the HIPAA Privacy Rule allows providers to leave appointment reminders for patients, as long as they limit the information disclosed (HHS HIPAA FAQ). This is general information, not legal advice; confirm your own setup with a compliance advisor.

Tips that make reminders work better

  • Keep it to one text. Around 160 characters keeps the message readable at a glance.
  • Use the patient’s first name and your practice name, so it doesn’t look like spam.
  • Make the reply do something. A “C” should mark the appointment confirmed and an “R” should start a reschedule, without the front desk retyping anything.
  • Send at sensible hours. Daytime sends, in the patient’s time zone.
  • Stop when they act. Once a patient confirms or reschedules, the rest of the sequence should stop.
