Dental Recall Guide

Dental patient reactivation
text templates

15 short, ready-to-send texts that bring overdue and lapsed patients back into your chairs, plus when to send them and how to automate the whole sequence.

By Usman Ali, founder of CRM Flow Lab · Published November 3, 2026

Quick answer: A good dental reactivation text is under 160 characters, uses the patient’s first name, names the specific reason they’re due (cleaning, check-up or unfinished treatment), and ends with a one-tap booking link. Send a sequence of 3–4 messages over about three weeks, stop as soon as the patient books or replies, and always include an opt-out.

Every dental practice has a list of patients who were “due six months ago.” They didn’t leave because they were unhappy. Most simply forgot, got busy, or never got a reminder. That list is the cheapest source of booked appointments you have, because these people already know and trust you.

Below are 15 templates we use as starting points when we build recall and reactivation sequences in GoHighLevel. Swap in your practice name and booking link, and keep the tone the way your front desk actually talks.

Who counts as a “reactivation” patient?

Split your list before you send anything. One message to everyone reads like spam; three short segments read like a practice that pays attention.

  • Overdue hygiene (6–12 months): missed their regular cleaning and check-up.
  • Unscheduled treatment: had a treatment plan presented (crown, implant, aligners) and never booked it.
  • Lapsed (12–24+ months): haven’t visited in over a year. These need the softest touch.

Overdue hygiene recall texts

1. The simple reminder

Hi {first_name}, it’s {practice}. You’re due for your cleaning and check-up. Grab a time that suits you here: {booking_link}

2. The time-saver

Hi {first_name}, booking your next cleaning takes about 30 seconds online: {booking_link}. Early morning and lunchtime slots are open this week. – {practice}

3. The gentle second nudge

Hi {first_name}, just following up on your check-up at {practice}. Want us to hold a spot for you? Reply YES and we’ll call you, or book here: {booking_link}

4. The insurance reminder (US, for patients with benefits)

Hi {first_name}, many dental plans reset in January, so unused cleaning benefits may not carry over. Book before year end: {booking_link} – {practice}

5. The last-call message

Hi {first_name}, this is our last reminder about your overdue check-up. If you’ve moved or changed dentists, reply STOP and we won’t message again. Otherwise: {booking_link}

Unscheduled treatment follow-up texts

These patients already said yes in the chair, then life got in the way. Keep it helpful, never pushy, and don’t put clinical details in a text.

6. The open-question check-in

Hi {first_name}, it’s {practice}. Do you have any questions about the treatment Dr. {doctor} recommended? Happy to help: just reply here.

7. The scheduling help

Hi {first_name}, we’ve kept your treatment plan on file. When you’re ready, you can pick a time here: {booking_link}. Payment options are available if that helps.

8. The “before it gets worse” nudge

Hi {first_name}, small dental issues are usually quicker and cheaper to treat early. Want to get your appointment on the calendar? {booking_link} – {practice}

9. The personal touch

Hi {first_name}, {staff_name} here from {practice}. I’ve got a couple of openings next week that would work for your appointment. Want me to save one for you?

10. The close-the-loop text

Hi {first_name}, we don’t want to keep messaging if now isn’t the right time. Reply LATER and we’ll check back in 3 months, or book here: {booking_link}

Lapsed patient win-back texts (12+ months)

11. The “we miss you” opener

Hi {first_name}, it’s been a while since we’ve seen you at {practice}! We’d love to get you back in for a check-up. Book online: {booking_link}

12. The what’s-new message

Hi {first_name}, {practice} now offers online booking and evening appointments. Your next check-up is a few taps away: {booking_link}

13. The new-patient-style offer (if your practice runs one)

Hi {first_name}, welcome back offer: {offer_details} for returning patients this month. Book here: {booking_link} – {practice}

14. The update-your-details text

Hi {first_name}, we’re updating our records at {practice}. Are you still in the area? Reply YES and we’ll help you book your next visit.

15. The final, respectful goodbye

Hi {first_name}, we’ll stop reminders for now. If you ever need us, booking is always open here: {booking_link}. Take care! – {practice}

When to send each message

StepTimingChannelGoal
1Day 0SMSSimple reminder + booking link
2Day 3EmailSame reminder with more detail and practice photos
3Day 7SMSSecond nudge with a reply option
4Day 14–21SMSLast call with clear opt-out

Send texts during business hours in the patient’s time zone (roughly 9am–7pm on weekdays), and stop the sequence the moment a patient books, replies or opts out. Nothing annoys a patient faster than a reminder for an appointment they booked yesterday.

Compliance basics before you hit send

  • Consent: only text patients who gave you their number and agreed to receive messages. Keep that opt-in on record.
  • Opt-out: include “Reply STOP to opt out” in the first message of every sequence, and honour it automatically.
  • Registration: in the US, business texting numbers need A2P 10DLC registration or carriers will filter your messages.
  • Privacy: keep diagnoses and clinical details out of texts. “Your treatment” is fine; naming the procedure or condition is not a good idea. Check HIPAA (US) or your local privacy rules with your compliance advisor.

How to automate this in GoHighLevel

  1. Import or sync your patient list with a “last visit” date and tag each contact by segment (hygiene due, treatment pending, lapsed).
  2. Build one workflow per segment using the timing table above, with SMS and email steps and wait steps between them.
  3. Add a goal event so the workflow ends as soon as an appointment is booked or the contact replies.
  4. Route replies to your front desk inbox so a human answers questions the same day.
  5. Track bookings from each workflow so you know which templates actually work, then rewrite the weakest one each month.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

A dental patient reactivation campaign is a sequence of texts, emails and calls sent to patients who are overdue for a visit or haven’t booked recommended treatment, with the goal of getting them back on the schedule. It targets people who already know your practice, so it usually costs far less per booking than finding new patients.

Three to four messages over two to three weeks is a sensible sequence: a first reminder, an email follow-up, a second text with a reply option and a final message with a clear opt-out. Stop immediately when the patient books, replies or opts out.

Weekday business hours in the patient’s time zone work best, roughly 9am to 7pm. Avoid early mornings, late nights and Sundays, and follow any quiet-hours rules that apply where your patients live.

Appointment reminders and recall texts can be sent compliantly when the patient has agreed to receive texts and the message avoids clinical details. Use a texting platform that supports your compliance needs and confirm your setup with your own compliance advisor.
