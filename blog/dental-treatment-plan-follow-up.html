Dental Treatment Plan Guide

Dental treatment plan
follow-up

Why treatment plans stall, a simple follow-up sequence, six texts you can adapt, and how to automate the whole thing.

By Usman Ali, founder of CRM Flow Lab · Published November 11, 2026

Quick answer: Dental treatment plan follow-up means contacting patients who were given a plan but have not booked it. Send a short check-in soon after the visit, then a few spaced messages that answer questions and offer a booking link, and stop as soon as the patient books or declines. Automating the sequence keeps it from depending on busy front-desk staff.

A treatment plan only helps the patient once it is scheduled. Many plans stall because the patient had a question they did not ask, needed time to think, or simply got busy. Without a follow-up, nobody notices until the patient is overdue for something else.

Below is a simple way to follow up, six texts you can adapt, and what to automate. We don’t quote conversion figures here, because they depend on your practice and patients.

Why do patients not book a treatment plan?

  • Cost or insurance questions they did not feel comfortable raising in the chair.
  • Uncertainty about what the treatment involves or whether it is urgent.
  • Timing. Work, family and travel push the booking to “later”.
  • Nobody followed up. The plan was presented, the patient left, and the next step was never owned by anyone.

What does a good treatment plan follow-up sequence look like?

  1. Day of or day after the visit: a warm check-in that thanks the patient and offers to answer questions.
  2. A few days later: a message that makes booking easy, with a link to your online booking page.
  3. About a week or two later: a gentle reminder that mentions the plan in general terms.
  4. A final message: a polite last note that leaves the door open, then stop.

Keep the spacing to what suits your practice, and stop the sequence the moment the patient books, replies or opts out.

What treatment plan follow-up texts can you copy?

Replace the bracketed parts with your own details. Keep clinical specifics out of texts.

1. Check-in after the visit.
“Hi [First name], thanks for coming in to [Practice name]. If you have any questions about your plan, just reply here and we’ll help.”

2. Easy booking.
“Hi [First name], when you’re ready to schedule the next step, you can pick a time here: [link]. Reply if you’d rather we call.”

3. Questions invited.
“Hi [First name], it’s common to have questions about timing or coverage. We’re happy to talk them through. Want us to call you?”

4. Gentle reminder.
“Hi [First name], just a friendly nudge from [Practice name] about your visit. We have openings coming up: [link].”

5. Final note.
“Hi [First name], we don’t want to crowd your inbox, so this is our last message about your plan. Whenever you’re ready, we’re here: [link].”

6. Reply handler.
“Thanks [First name]! A team member will get back to you shortly to help with that.”

What should you check before sending these messages?

  • Consent. Make sure patients agreed to receive texts, and honor opt-outs straight away.
  • Privacy. Keep health details and billing figures out of texts. Check the rules that apply to your practice with your own advisor.
  • A human handoff. When a patient replies with a question, a person should answer.
  • Tone. Helpful, not pushy. The goal is to remove friction, not pressure anyone.

How do you automate treatment plan follow-up?

Manual follow-up depends on someone remembering to check a list. With a CRM the sequence runs on its own:

  • Trigger when a treatment plan is marked as presented.
  • Scheduled messages go out by text or email at the spacing you choose.
  • Automatic stop when the patient books, replies or opts out.
  • Staff alert when a patient replies so a person can step in.
  • Reporting on which plans are still open, so nothing is forgotten.
