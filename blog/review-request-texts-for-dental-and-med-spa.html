Review Generation Guide

Review request texts
for dental & med spa

When to ask, six texts you can adapt, how to handle unhappy patients, and how to automate the whole thing.

By Usman Ali, founder of CRM Flow Lab · Published November 11, 2026

Quick answer: Ask for a review right after the visit with a short, personal text that includes one direct link to your Google review page. Give every patient the same link, offer anyone who was unhappy a private line to the owner or manager, and automate the request so it goes out after every appointment without anyone remembering to send it.

Most happy patients never leave a review, simply because nobody asked at a good moment. A simple text sent soon after the appointment fixes that. We don’t quote review-count or rating figures here, because results depend on your practice and patients.

When should you ask for a review?

  • Right after the visit. The experience is fresh and the patient is still thinking about it.
  • After a completed appointment only. Not after a no-show, a cancellation or a visit that went wrong.
  • Once, plus one gentle reminder. Then stop. Repeated asks annoy patients.

What review request texts can you copy?

Replace the bracketed parts with your own details. Use the same direct Google review link for everyone.

1. Dental, after a visit.
“Hi [First name], thanks for visiting [Practice name] today. If you have a minute, we’d love your honest feedback: [Google review link]”

2. Med spa, after a treatment.
“Hi [First name], thank you for coming in to [Spa name]. We hope you enjoyed your visit. Would you share your experience? [Google review link]”

3. Gentle reminder.
“Hi [First name], just a quick reminder in case you missed our note. Your feedback helps other patients find us: [Google review link]”

4. Private line for any concern.
“Hi [First name], if anything about your visit wasn’t right, please reply here or contact [Manager name] directly so we can make it right.”

5. Thank-you after a review.
“Thank you so much, [First name]! We really appreciate you taking the time.”

6. Reply handler.
“Thanks [First name], we’re sorry to hear that. A team member will contact you shortly to help.”

How do you handle unhappy patients?

Our approach is simple: everyone gets the same Google review link, and anyone who tells you the visit wasn’t great also gets a private line to you, so you can make it right. Do not hide the review link from some patients, and do not offer rewards for reviews. Check the rules of the review platform you use and the rules that apply to your practice.

What should you check before sending these messages?

  • Consent. Make sure patients agreed to receive texts, and honor opt-outs straight away.
  • Privacy. Do not mention treatments or health details in the text. Check your obligations with your own advisor.
  • A human handoff. When a patient replies with a concern, a person should answer.
  • Reply to reviews. A short, polite response shows future patients you listen.

How do you automate review requests?

Manual requests depend on someone remembering to send them. With a CRM the process runs on its own:

  • Trigger when an appointment is marked as completed.
  • Automatic request goes out by text at the timing you choose.
  • One reminder, then the sequence stops when the patient reviews, replies or opts out.
  • Staff alert when a patient reports a problem so a person can step in.
  • Review monitoring so new reviews are seen and answered.
